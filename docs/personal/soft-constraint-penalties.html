How each soft constraint calculates its penalty

Each section explains the current solver behavior, the settings it receives, and one possible way to give the solver a single penalty between 0 and 100.

Two different designs appear on this page. “Current calculation” describes the code today. “Suggestion” is an idea for discussion, not implemented behavior or a finalized formula. Today, the profile weight multiplies the current penalty terms. The proposed model uses one combined soft_constraint_penalty per constraint and a profile-supplied weight from 0 to 10, with default 1 and 0 disabling its contribution. Weights are to be rounded up to one decimal place.

Inputs below list constraint-specific settings and relevant extra data. All constraints also receive the shared solver context. Unit examples use the common convention 10 project units = 1 metre. Initial weights shown are current values, not the proposed scale.

Preferred room neighbours

room_relations · initial weight 40

What it does

Encourages rooms with soft relationships to share enough wall. An AND relationship wants every listed neighbour; an OR relationship wants at least one.

Current calculation

Each missed AND target adds 1; a missed OR group adds 1. The solver sums these terms with the profile weight. A satisfied check adds 0.

Specific inputs

Soft room relations, their targets and AND/OR policy; minimum_overlap, set to 10 units (1 m) in the initial profile.

Suggested 0–100 penalty

Use 100 × missed checks ÷ applicable checks. Treat each AND target and each OR group as one check. With no applicable checks, contribute 0. This makes all missed checks 100.

Room size relationships

room_size_relations · initial weight 1

What it does

Prefers certain types to be no larger than others. For example, bathrooms are preferred no larger than bedrooms, and bedrooms no larger than living rooms.

Current calculation

For each applicable bound, it compares room-area extremes, measures the excess or shortfall, normalizes against the reference room’s maximum area capacity, and rounds up at configured precision. Each term has a rule multiplier; default rules use 20. Upper bounds compare the largest compared room with the smallest reference room.

Specific inputs

rules define compared and reference types, minimum and/or maximum ratios, and optional rule multipliers. precision defaults to 1000. A rule is skipped if a type is absent.

Suggested 0–100 penalty

For each applicable bound, express its violation relative to a documented reference area, cap it at 100, then average the bound penalties. Define the saturation point with examples before adopting it; averaging changes how multiple violations compete.

Position of the room group

floor_cluster_position · initial weight 1

What it does

Can centre the whole room group left to right and pull its front edge toward the front. The initial profile enables centering only.

Current calculation

Horizontal penalty is the absolute difference between the left and right margins. Front penalty is the group’s front-edge distance from the floor front. Each has its own multiplier; both terms receive the profile weight.

Specific inputs

horizontal_multiplier and front_multiplier default to 1 in the constraint; initial profile sets 1 and 0. excluded_room_types defaults to none.

Suggested 0–100 penalty

Divide margin difference by floor width and front distance by floor length. Convert each to 0–100 and take a weighted average of enabled components. A multiplier of 0 excludes that component; no enabled component gives 0.

Empty space inside the room group

dead_space · initial weight 3

What it does

Encourages the rooms to form a compact group with fewer gaps inside the rectangle around them.

Current calculation

bounding rectangle area − sum of room areas

It produces one area term. Empty floor outside that rectangle is not counted.

Specific inputs

No constraint-specific settings. The current implementation ignores settings.

Suggested 0–100 penalty

Use 100 × empty area ÷ bounding rectangle area. Zero means no internal empty area; values approach 100 as the rectangle becomes empty. Define zero-area handling safely. This changes the preference from absolute gap area to a proportion.

Hallway efficiency

hallway_efficiency · initial weight 1

What it does

Encourages smaller hallways and hallway walls shared with other rooms. Supplied hallways remain required.

Current calculation

total hallway area × area multiplier + sum of exposed hallway wall lengths

Area is one term and each hallway’s unshared perimeter is another. The solver applies the profile weight to each.

Specific inputs

hallway_room_types selects included types; area_penalty_multiplier defaults to 1 and can be 0. No hallway means no penalty terms.

Suggested 0–100 penalty

Combine hallway area as a fraction of floor area with unshared hallway wall as a fraction of total hallway perimeter. Use a documented balance between the two, then multiply by 100. No hallway gives 0. Decide the balance by reviewing plans.

Bathroom position

bathroom_depth · initial weight 2

What it does

Prefers bathrooms farther from the front of the floor. It includes regular and attached bathrooms by default.

Current calculation

For each selected bathroom, the penalty is twice the distance from its centre to the back edge: 2 × floor length − (2 × y + room length). The terms are summed with the profile weight.

Specific inputs

room_types selects the room types; by default it includes bathroom and attached bathroom.

Suggested 0–100 penalty

Average each selected room’s centre-to-back distance divided by floor length, then multiply by 100. A centre at the back gives 0; at the front gives 100. No selected rooms gives 0.

Exterior wall exposure

exterior_wall_exposure · initial weight 10

What it does

Prefers specified rooms to have clear outward paths from selected walls. The initial rule wants at least one kitchen with 10 units (1 m) of continuous back exposure.

Current calculation

Each applicable rule adds 0 if satisfied or 1 if missed, regardless of how many rooms miss it. A rule with no matching rooms adds no term. Rooms block the outward paths; empty space does not.

Specific inputs

rules specify room types, all/at-least matching, sides, full-wall or minimum continuous length, and nested all/any requirements.

Suggested 0–100 penalty

Use 100 × missed applicable rules ÷ applicable rules. With no applicable rules, contribute 0. One missed kitchen rule would therefore give 100. Decide whether a partly satisfied all-room rule should still count as fully missed.

Room centre line

room_center_line · optional, not in initial profile

What it does

Prefers each room’s centre near the floor’s left-to-right centre line. This can compete with other placement rules because rooms cannot overlap.

Current calculation

For each room, it adds abs(2 × x + room width − floor width), which is twice the distance from the room centre to the centre line.

Specific inputs

No constraint-specific settings are used.

Suggested 0–100 penalty

Average each room’s current distance expression divided by floor width, then multiply by 100. A room centred on the line gives 0; the theoretical extreme gives 100. With no rooms, contribute 0.

Stay close to the seed

seed_stability · refinement, not initial profile

What it does

Prefers rooms to stay near their supplied seed positions and sizes. It is used by the built-in refinement profiles.

Current calculation

For each matched seed room, it adds absolute X and Y movement times the position multiplier, plus absolute width and length changes times the size multiplier. Size changes are included only when the seed provides that dimension. No seed produces no terms.

Specific inputs

A seed layout and position_multiplier / size_multiplier, each defaulting to 1 in the constraint. Separate seed-policy tolerances can also bound movement and size as hard limits.

Suggested 0–100 penalty

Normalize each available position and size difference against a documented reference distance, average the enabled components and matched rooms, and cap at 100. No matching seed gives 0. Choose reference distances carefully so ordinary adjustments are visible.

Suggestions require calibration. A common range makes weights easier to understand, but the meaning of 100 still depends on the chosen formula and feasible layouts.
