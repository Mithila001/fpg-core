Initial generation constraints

A practical reminder of what the floor-plan solver must obey and what it tries to improve when it creates the first layout.

Hard constraints = must pass

Every returned solution must satisfy these rules together. If they cannot all fit, the solver may report an infeasible result.

Soft constraints = preferences

The solver adds up penalties and tries to reduce them. A preference may be missed when another preference or a hard rule wins.

Reading the numbers: Values below are project units. Using the project's common example of 10 units = 1 metre, 10 units = 1 m, 12 = 1.2 m, 30 = 3 m and 40 = 4 m. Area conversion is squared: 100 square units = 1 m². The application owns its unit conversion; these are examples, not automatic conversions.

Scope: built-in INITIAL_GENERATION_PROFILE and the solver's structural rules. Custom profiles, input room sizes and relations can change the outcome. This is the solver stage; candidate search, later refinement, openings and post-processing have their own behavior.

Hard constraints

All supplied rooms are required. These rules are applied together, even if that leaves no possible layout.

Rooms fit and do not overlap

Each supplied rectangular room stays inside the floor boundary, keeps its specified width and area limits, and cannot overlap another room. Width limits respect the specified axis. These are built into the model, even if optional constraints are changed.

Structural model invariants

Room shape

For most rooms, X width ÷ Y length must be 0.60–1.80. Garages use 0.45–0.70; verandas use 1.20–3.50. Hallways follow their own dimensions instead. These ratios are directional, not simply short side ÷ long side.

aspect_ratio

Required neighbours

Relationships marked hard in the input must share at least 10 units (1 m) of wall. An AND relationship needs all listed neighbours; an OR relationship needs at least one. A corner touch is not enough.

room_relations

Attached bathrooms

Each attached bathroom must share at least 10 units (1 m) of wall with exactly one bedroom; each bedroom can pair with at most one attached bathroom. This is a wall relationship, not a guarantee of a door.

attached_bathroom_pairing

Minimum floor coverage

The combined room area must cover at least 60% of the generation floor area. This does not mean every empty gap must be filled.

minimum_coverage

Hallway connections

Each hallway must share at least 10 units (1 m) with a living room and also with a room that is neither a hallway nor a living room. If the necessary room types are absent, a supplied hallway makes the model infeasible.

hallway_connectivity

Hallway size

The corridor width is exactly 10 units (1 m). Its long dimension cannot exceed half the span of the non-hallway rooms along the same axis. Room size bounds also apply.

hallway_dimensions

Hallway overlap limits

Same-orientation hallways may overlap in projection by at most 40 units (4 m) horizontally or 30 units (3 m) vertically, even when separated by a gap. If two hallways touch, their actual shared wall is separately limited to 12 units (1.2 m); a corner touch has zero shared length.

hallway_parallel_overlap · hallway_shared_wall

Regular bathroom beside hallways

One side of a regular bathroom may share any length with hallways. Across its other sides, the combined hallway contact may be no more than 10 units (1 m). Contact with multiple hallways is added together. Attached bathrooms are not covered by this rule.

bathroom_hallway_shared_wall

Required outside exposure

At least one hallway or kitchen needs a continuous 10-unit (1 m) clear segment toward the back. Every garage needs its whole front wall and either its whole left or right wall exposed. Every veranda needs its whole front wall exposed. If there is no veranda, every living room needs its whole front wall exposed.

exterior_wall_exposure · living_room_front

Soft preferences

Weights are shown for the initial profile. They multiply different kinds of penalties, so a larger weight alone does not tell you which preference always wins.

Preferred neighbours weight 40

Tries to satisfy input relationships marked soft using at least 10 units (1 m) of shared wall. AND counts missed neighbours separately; OR needs any one of the listed rooms.

room_relations

Room size ordering weight 1

Prefers regular and attached bathrooms no larger than bedrooms, living rooms, dining rooms, kitchens or garages. Also prefers bedrooms and kitchens no larger than living rooms. It compares the largest room of the first type against the smallest of the second; absent types are skipped. This is a preference, not a guarantee.

room_size_relations · default rules have multiplier 20

Centre the room group weight 1

Tries to balance empty space to the left and right of the whole room cluster. It does not centre every room. The default initial profile does not pull the cluster towards the front.

floor_cluster_position · horizontal 1, front 0

Reduce gaps in the layout weight 3

Penalizes empty area inside the smallest rectangle surrounding all rooms. Empty space outside that rectangle is not included in this particular penalty.

dead_space

Efficient hallways weight 1

Penalizes hallway area and hallway wall length not shared with rooms. This encourages compact, enclosed hallways; it does not remove supplied hallways. There is no separate preferred-length penalty in the current objective.

hallway_efficiency · area multiplier 1

Bathrooms farther back weight 2

Prefers regular and attached bathrooms farther from the front of the floor, based on their centre position. Other rules may place them closer to the front.

bathroom_depth

Kitchen exposure at the back weight 10

Prefers at least one kitchen to have a continuous 10-unit (1 m) clear segment towards the back. If there is no kitchen, this preference is skipped. The separate hard back-exposure rule may instead be met by a hallway.

exterior_wall_exposure

What “exposed” means

Imagine looking straight out from a room wall toward the corresponding edge of the generation boundary. Other rooms block that view; empty space does not. The room can be set back from the boundary and still be exposed.

A minimum-length rule needs one continuous clear segment. A full-wall rule needs the whole selected wall clear. For a garage, the side check covers its own wall projection, not an extended strip beyond it.

Exposure at this stage does not promise a window or door and may change after later geometry processing. Front is the solver's Y-min side; back is Y-max.

Useful boundaries to remember

Available, but not enabled here

boundary_placement can explicitly require boundary contact; room_size_hierarchy can enforce size ordering as a hard rule. room_center_line is an optional soft preference. seed_stability is used in refinement, not this initial profile.

Hints and time limit

Candidate positions are optional hints, not fixed placements. The default time limit is 5 seconds. A feasible result respects the hard rules but need not be the best possible preference score; an unknown result is not proof that no solution exists.

Defaults are starting points

Room specifications, relation strengths, profile settings and consumer configuration can change the actual rules or values. A conflict between valid-looking inputs may make the full model infeasible.
