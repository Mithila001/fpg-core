# Lab UI standards

## Required

Keep interaction states legible, including selection, focus, disabled controls,
errors and progress. Distinguish room categories with consistent colors and
readable labels or a legend. Give destructive actions a consistent danger style.
Use the shared button roles for primary, secondary and destructive actions.
Confirm a changed surface is usable with one deliberate UI self-review.

## Starting points

Use a dominant left workspace with narrower right context, compact top or view
controls, and a readable legend where useful. Adapt the layout to non-canvas
experiments and configuration pages. Keep the object of inspection prominent.
Show frequently selected records directly when space permits. Use secondary
dialogs or menus for uncommon actions. Use short labels, remove redundant
explanations, and put detailed guidance close to the decision it supports.

## Examples and review

E02B uses a plan workspace with measurement context. E03A uses tables and a
relationship matrix for analysis. Check edited pages at typical desktop widths
such as 1440 and 1280 pixels. Review the primary action, selection and feedback,
visible room distinctions where applicable, and whether text earns its space.
